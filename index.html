Meet Katherine

A return to
the body.

“The earth was holding me for what seemed like the first time.”

My path to Ayurveda began with a diagnosis. A week before the world shut down in 2020, I learned I had PCOS. At the time I was a high-achieving student and rower, running on discipline and people-pleasing, my nervous system humming in fight-or-flight since a chaotic childhood. But underneath it all was a kid who used to climb trees and lose hours in the park across the street, someone who knew, once, how to simply be in her body.

I spent years working as an EMT, and found a front-row seat to how disconnected modern medicine is from the root causes of disease. Burnt out and heartbroken by what I'd seen, I left that work for a degree in developmental psychology, and food became my medicine. I completed my 200-hour yoga teacher training and moved to a village in Sri Lanka with no electricity, where Ayurveda and yoga weren't a wellness trend but simply how people lived. I learned my prakruti and vikruti, my constitution and my imbalance, and felt the earth holding me for what seemed like the first time.

The years since have been an apprenticeship: children's yoga certification, research on yoga and preschoolers, a month of silence in the Amazon, a deep dive into gut health. I completed a year-long program at the Sarasvati School of Ayurveda and Yoga Therapy under Susan Bass, earning my certification as an Ayurvedic Wellness Advisor alongside my 500-hour yoga certification.
